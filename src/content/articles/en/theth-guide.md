Theth is a scattered village of stone houses at the end of a long valley in the Albanian Alps, ringed by some of the steepest limestone walls in the Balkans. Most Peaks of the Balkans hikers pass through it on the way to or from Valbonë, but it deserves a rest day of its own.

## Theth's highlights at a glance

| Sight | Time from the village centre | Cost | Good to know |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blue Eye of Theth (Kaprre) | 2–2.5 h each way on foot; 1–1.5 h return from Nderlysaj | free; 4x4 to Nderlysaj about €5 per person one way | Ice-cold pool under a waterfall; small café on site in summer |
| Grunas waterfall | 30–45 min each way | free | About 30 m high; signed path after the bridge |
| Grunas canyon | 20–30 min | free | Narrow gorge with walls up to about 60 m |
| Lock-in tower (Kulla e Ngujimit) | in the village | about 150 lek / €1.50 | No fixed hours: ask at the nearby houses |
| Theth church | in the village | free | Built in 1892; often only open around Sunday services |
| Nderlysaj river pools | 1.5–2 h on foot or a short drive | free | Popular for a swim on hot days |

## The Blue Eye of Theth

The most popular half-day walk. From the village you follow the valley road south to Nderlysaj, cross the river and climb about 450 m to a deep turquoise pool fed by a small waterfall. The water rarely gets above 10 °C, but on a hot afternoon a quick dip is the reason many people come.

To save legs before or after a long trail stage, take a 4x4 taxi to Nderlysaj (about €5 per person one way in recent seasons, arranged through your guesthouse) and walk only the last part. Drivers with their own car have reported a parking charge of about 200 lek at Nderlysaj. On the way back, the rock pools along the river are a good place to stop. The path is busy in July and August, so start early or go late in the afternoon.

## Grunas waterfall and canyon

The shortest outing in Theth and ideal for an arrival afternoon. A signed path leads from the village to the canyon, where the river has cut a narrow gorge, and then on to the waterfall, about 30 m high. Allow 1.5–2 hours there and back with time to look around.

## The lock-in tower and the church

The **lock-in tower** (Kulla e Ngujimit) is a fortified stone house where men caught up in a blood feud under the Kanun, the old customary law of northern Albania, could shelter while elders negotiated. Inside you can see the narrow rifle slits and the cramped rooms. A caretaker family opens it on request for a small fee (about 150 lek or €1.50 in recent reports).

The little **stone church** in the middle of the valley, built in 1892, is the classic Theth photo, with the peaks rising behind it. It is often closed outside services, but the view from the meadows around it is the point.

## Day hikes from Theth

- **Towards the Valbona Pass.** If you are not crossing to Valbonë, walk up the switchbacks for the view over the valley and come back the same way. For the full crossing, see [how hard the Theth to Valbonë hike is](/en/theth-to-valbona/).
- **Qafa e Thorës.** A quieter climb to a pass at about 1,630 m on the old road towards Boga, with wide views back over Theth.
- **Along the valley.** An easy stroll between the scattered hamlets, ideal for a rest day after a long stage.

## Practical tips

- **Season.** Theth is busiest from mid-June to late September. Most guesthouses close in winter, when snow can block the road.
- **Getting there.** The road from Shkodër is now asphalted but narrow and steep; shared minibuses run in summer. See [how to get to Theth](/en/getting-to-theth/).
- **Where to sleep.** Theth has dozens of family guesthouses, usually with half board. Book ahead for July and August. More in [where to sleep on the Peaks of the Balkans](/en/peaks-of-the-balkans-accommodation/).
- **Cash.** Bring lek or euros; card payment is rare in the village.
