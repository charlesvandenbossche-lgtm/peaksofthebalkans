The Peaks of the Balkans opened in 2012 as a cross-border trail meant to bring hikers – and income – to remote mountain villages. It links shepherd settlements, high passes and glacial lakes across three countries, and it is now one of the most popular multi-day treks in Europe outside the Alps. The best-known section, Theth to Valbonë, sees tens of thousands of hikers every summer.

## The trail in numbers

| | |
|---|---|
| Distance | about 192 km (official figure; GPS tracks vary) |
| Stages | 10, usually 13–22 km each |
| Total ascent | about 9,800 m |
| Highest points on the main route | passes and ridges between 1,700 and 2,300 m |
| Countries | Albania, Kosovo, Montenegro |
| Season | late June – September (mid-June to mid-October in good years) |
| Accommodation | guesthouses and summer shepherd huts, usually half board |
| Permits | border permit for each country, apply about 15 days ahead |

## Where does it start?

The trail is a loop, so you can join it anywhere with road access. The most common starting points are:

- **Theth (Albania)**, the classic start, walking anticlockwise towards Valbonë on day 1.
- **Valbonë (Albania)**, often reached via the Komani Lake ferry.
- **Plav or Vusanje (Montenegro)**, a good option if you fly into Podgorica.
- **Drelaj or Rekë e Allagës near Peja (Kosovo)**, if you arrive from Pristina.

See [getting to Theth](/en/getting-to-theth/) for transport and the [stage-by-stage overview](/en/peaks-of-the-balkans-stages/) for the full route.

## The 10 stages at a glance

| # | Stage | Country |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Theth → Valbonë | Albania |
| 2 | Valbonë → Çerem | Albania |
| 3 | Çerem → Dobërdol | Albania |
| 4 | Dobërdol → Milishevc | Albania → Kosovo |
| 5 | Milishevc → Rekë e Allagës | Kosovo |
| 6 | Rekë e Allagës → Drelaj | Kosovo |
| 7 | Drelaj → Babino Polje | Kosovo → Montenegro |
| 8 | Babino Polje → Plav (via Hrid Lake) | Montenegro |
| 9 | Plav → Vusanje | Montenegro |
| 10 | Vusanje → Theth (via the Peja Pass) | Montenegro → Albania |

## What you need to arrange

1. **Border permits.** Walking from one country into another away from an official border post requires a permit from each country's border police. See [permits](/en/peaks-of-the-balkans-permits/).
2. **Beds.** Guesthouses fill up in July and August; book the busy villages (Theth, Valbonë, Vusanje) in advance. See [where to sleep](/en/peaks-of-the-balkans-accommodation/).
3. **Navigation.** The waymarking is good in Albania and Montenegro but patchy in parts of Kosovo. Carry an offline GPX track.
4. **Cash.** Many guesthouses take cash only, and there are few ATMs along the route (Plav is the main one).

## How difficult is it?

It is a demanding but non-technical trek: long days, steep climbs of 800–1,200 m, rocky descents and sometimes snowfields in early summer. Good fitness and some multi-day hiking experience help. See [how hard is the Peaks of the Balkans](/en/how-hard-is-peaks-of-the-balkans/).

## Do you have to walk the whole loop?

No. Many hikers walk 5 to 7 days and skip the long or less scenic sections with a local transfer. The Theth – Valbonë crossing, the Dobërdol plateau, Hrid Lake and the Ropojana valley to Vusanje are the highlights most people want to include. Organised trips such as [The Balkan Trail](https://thebalkantrail.com) follow exactly that idea: the best sections on foot, the permits and transfers taken care of.
