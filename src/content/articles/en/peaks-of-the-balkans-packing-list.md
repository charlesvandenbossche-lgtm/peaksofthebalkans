Most hikers carry far too much. On the Peaks of the Balkans you sleep and eat in guesthouses, so your pack can stay small – unless you camp.

## The essentials

| Category | Item | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Documents | Passport, printed border permits, travel insurance | Border patrols check permits |
| Money | Cash in euros (and some lek) | Few ATMs, cards rarely accepted |
| Navigation | Offline map app with GPX track, phone, power bank | Patchy waymarking in Kosovo |
| Footwear | Broken-in hiking boots or sturdy trail shoes | Rocky, steep paths |
| Poles | Trekking poles | Long descents |
| Water | 2 l capacity, filter or tablets | Springs are common but not always reliable |
| Sun | Hat, sunglasses, sunscreen SPF 50 | Little shade on high pastures |

## Clothing

- Quick-dry T-shirts (2) and hiking shorts or trousers
- Warm mid-layer (fleece or light down)
- Waterproof jacket; waterproof trousers in June and September
- Warm hat and light gloves for passes and cold mornings
- Hiking socks (3 pairs)
- Something clean and comfortable for the evenings

## If you camp

- Light tent, sleeping mat and a sleeping bag rated to about 5 °C
- Headlamp
- Earplugs: dogs and roosters start early

## What you can leave at home

- A stove and food for days (guesthouses cook)
- A full sleeping bag if you stay indoors: a liner is enough
- Heavy cameras and extra lenses: weight adds up on 1,000 m climbs

## Pack less, or carry less

Two things make the biggest difference: a smaller pack and someone else carrying the rest. With [The Balkan Trail](https://thebalkantrail.com) you can rent a tent, sleeping bag, mat or poles, and add luggage transport so you only walk with a daypack.
