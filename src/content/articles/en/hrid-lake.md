Hrid Lake (Hridsko jezero) is a small glacial lake at about 1,970 m in Prokletije National Park, in the far east of Montenegro. It lies under the rock walls of Hridski Krš (2,358 m), ringed by pine and spruce, and is often called the "lake of happiness": local legend says fairies bathed here, and visitors still throw in a coin and make a wish. For Peaks of the Balkans hikers it is the highlight of the day between Babino Polje and Plav.

## From Babino Polje: the short way

Babino Polje is a scattered summer settlement of meadows and eco-huts about 15 km (around 30 minutes by car) from Plav. The last part of the road is rough, so a 4x4 is the safer choice if you drive up.

From Babino Polje, follow the 4WD track north-east. After a few minutes a signpost points right towards Hrid Lake. The track climbs gently to **Katun Bajrovica**, a shepherd settlement where Samelova Koliba serves home-made food and coffee and has beds when it is open. From the katun a marked forest path (trail no. 542) climbs through pine and spruce to the lake shore. It is a moderate walk without technical sections, and you come back the same way.

## On the Peaks of the Balkans: Babino Polje to Plav

If you are walking the loop, the lake comes early in the Babino Polje–Plav stage:

1. **Babino Polje → Katun Bajrovica → Hrid Lake.** The same route as above, a good place for a long break and a swim.
2. **Over the ridge west of the lake.** The trail climbs about 450 m above the lake to the highest point of the day, around 2,050–2,070 m.
3. **Down to Treskavica.** A 4WD track leads to a timber shelter, then a forest path to the summer pastures of Treskavica, where shepherds sell cheese and other dairy products from roughly late June to late September.
4. **Down to Plav.** More track and path through forest and farmland bring you into Plav, the only real town on the loop, with shops and an ATM.

The descent is long (about 1,250 m in total), so keep some energy and water for the afternoon. If that is too much in one day, you can split the stage by sleeping at Treskavica, which gives two days of about 12 and 8 km. See how this day fits in a full trek in [the stage-by-stage guide](/en/peaks-of-the-balkans-stages/) or in [the 7-day itinerary](/en/peaks-of-the-balkans-in-7-days/).

## From Plav

There is no short hike to Hrid Lake from Plav itself. On foot you basically walk the Peaks of the Balkans stage in reverse, which means about 1,250 m of climbing before you reach the lake. A more relaxed option is to arrange a lift from Plav to Babino Polje (ask your guesthouse) and do the short walk from there. The local Hrid mountaineering club also runs a mountain hut at Maslo on the northern approach, which is open if you let the club know in advance.

## Swimming and the lake itself

The lake is about 300 m long, 160 m wide and only 5–6 m deep, and the water is so clear that you can see the stony bottom. In July and August a quick swim is one of the nicest moments of the whole trek, though the water stays cold. A few practical points:

- **No camping at the lake.** The lake and its surroundings are a strict nature reserve. Sleep in Babino Polje, at Katun Bajrovica or in Plav instead.
- **Bring insect repellent.** Midges and mosquitoes can be annoying on warm, still days.
- **Take your rubbish with you.** There are no bins at the lake.

## When can you go?

The walk is usually snow-free from late June to September. Snow patches can linger around the lake into early July after a snowy winter, and the katuns and shepherd huts are only occupied in summer. In autumn the larches and beech woods below the lake turn colour, but days are shorter and most mountain accommodation has closed by October.

## Practical tips

- **Permits.** You do not need a border permit for a day hike from Babino Polje, but you do if you arrive on the Peaks of the Balkans from Dobërdol over the three-border point. See [the permits guide](/en/peaks-of-the-balkans-permits/).
- **Water and food.** There are springs at Katun Bajrovica and along the route, but carry enough water for the long descent to Plav, and buy lunch at the katun if it is open.
- **Cash.** Plav has the only ATM on this part of the loop; pay cash in Babino Polje and at the katuns.
- **Navigation.** The Peaks of the Balkans route is marked with red-and-white waymarks, but carry an offline map or GPX track for the forest sections.
