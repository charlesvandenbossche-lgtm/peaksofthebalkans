This is the most walked section of the Peaks of the Balkans, and many people do it as a stand-alone hike, often combined with the Komani Lake ferry.

## The route in numbers

| | |
|---|---|
| Distance | 14–17 km (depending on where you start and finish in the valleys) |
| Climb | ± 1,000 m |
| Descent | ± 900 m |
| Highest point | Valbona Pass, ± 1,800 m |
| Walking time | 6–7 h, plus breaks |
| Difficulty | moderate to hard, non-technical |
| Water | springs on the Theth side; carry at least 1.5 l |

## Step by step from Theth

1. **Theth village → forest (1–1.5 h).** A gentle start through the valley, then into the beech forest.
2. **Switchbacks to the pass (2–2.5 h).** The steepest part: a long climb on stony switchbacks, partly in the open.
3. **Valbona Pass.** Views over both valleys. In summer, a small café hut nearby sells drinks.
4. **Descent to Rragam (1.5–2 h).** Steep, loose and hard on the knees. Poles help.
5. **Rragam → Valbonë (1 h).** Along a dry riverbed or gravel road; in summer you can often catch a lift or a minibus.

## Which direction?

Theth → Valbonë is the classic direction and has the steeper climb. Valbonë → Theth starts with a longer, more gradual walk-in and a steep descent into Theth. Both are fine; in summer, walking from Valbonë means you often have the morning light on the Theth side.

## Avoiding the crowds

In July and August hundreds of people walk this stage on the same day. Start at 6:30–7:00, or walk it on a weekday in June or September.

## Getting back or onwards

There is no road between the two valleys. To return, you either walk back, take the long road around (4–5 hours), or continue via Komani Lake. Many hikers arrange their luggage to be sent ahead by car or mule. [The Balkan Trail](https://thebalkantrail.com) starts with this crossing, with luggage transport available and a transfer arranged on the other side.
