There are three ways to walk the Peaks of the Balkans, and the right one depends less on fitness than on how much organising you want to do.

## The three options

| | Fully independent | Self-guided package | Guided or supported group |
|---|---|---|---|
| Permits | You apply to three border police | Arranged | Arranged |
| Accommodation | You book each guesthouse | Booked | Booked |
| Luggage | You carry it, or arrange transfers | Often included or optional | Often optional |
| Navigation | GPX and your own judgement | GPX and route notes | Guides on the route |
| Transfers | You arrange them | Usually included | Included |
| Flexibility | Total | Fixed itinerary | Fixed itinerary |
| Cost | Lowest | Medium | Medium to higher |

## When independent works well

- You have 10–12 days and want the full loop
- You like flexibility and changing plans on the go
- You are comfortable with navigation and remote terrain

## When an organised trip makes more sense

- You have one week and want the best sections, not every kilometre
- You don't want to deal with three permits and ten guesthouse phone calls
- You'd rather have someone around in the Kosovo sections or in bad weather
- You want your luggage moved and gear available to rent

## A middle way

[The Balkan Trail](https://thebalkantrail.com) sits between the two: a 7-day trip with five hiking days on the highlights, where you walk at your own pace with the route in an app, while guides walk ahead, behind and along the route. Permits, park fees and local transfers are included; luggage transport, meals and gear rental are optional.
