## The main risks

| Risk | When | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Getting lost | Kosovo stages, fog | Offline GPX track, don't rely on paint marks alone |
| Thunderstorms | July–August afternoons | Start by 7:00, be off ridges by early afternoon |
| Snow on passes | May–June | Ask locals, carry microspikes, turn back if unsure |
| Heat | July–August | 2 l of water, early starts, sun protection |
| Guard dogs | High pastures | See below |
| Injury far from roads | Whole route | Insurance that covers hiking, first-aid kit |

## Shepherd dogs

Large guard dogs protect the flocks on the summer pastures. They usually bark and approach but rarely attack. Stay calm, don't run, keep your poles between you and the dog, walk around the flock at a distance, and wait for the shepherd if a dog won't let you pass.

## Phone signal

Signal is good in villages and patchy or absent in many valleys and on parts of the plateaus. Albania, Kosovo and Montenegro are not part of EU roaming: buy a local SIM or an eSIM data plan.

## Emergency numbers

| Country | General | Police | Ambulance | Fire |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Albania | 112 | 129 | 127 | 128 |
| Kosovo | 112 | 192 | 194 | 193 |
| Montenegro | 112 | 122 | 124 | 123 |

## Water

Springs and taps along the route are generally used by locals and hikers, but quality isn't guaranteed. A small filter or purification tablets are a good idea, especially below pastures.

## Walking alone

Many people walk sections alone, especially Theth – Valbonë. On the quieter stages, tell your guesthouse where you are going and when you expect to arrive. If you'd rather not navigate the Kosovo stages on your own, an organised trip such as [The Balkan Trail](https://thebalkantrail.com) lets you walk at your own pace while guides walk ahead, behind and along the route.
