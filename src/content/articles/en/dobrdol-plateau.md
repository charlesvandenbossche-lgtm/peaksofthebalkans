Dobërdol (also written Doberdol) is one of the most remote places on the Peaks of the Balkans. There is no village in the usual sense: just a broad green valley, grazing cows and sheep, and a cluster of wooden summer huts under the border ridge. For most hikers it is the night between Çerem and Milishevc, and the last stop in Albania before the trail crosses into Kosovo.

## What is Dobërdol like?

Picture a high, open bowl of grass with rounded mountains on all sides and no trees. In summer the families of the area move up with their animals, as they have done for generations, and live in wooden huts scattered along the valley. A dirt track runs through the middle, but there is no regular road transport: goods and luggage come up by 4x4 or on horseback.

Life here is simple. Expect cows wandering past your window, fresh milk and cheese on the table, and a very dark, starry sky at night. Don't count on showers, phone signal or a place to charge your phone; ask your hosts what they have. In winter Dobërdol is deserted and cut off by snow.

## Where do you sleep in Dobërdol?

You sleep in one of the summer guesthouses run by the shepherd families. Names you will see are **Leonardi Guest House**, **Bashkimi** and **Bylbyl Vatnikaj**. What they offer:

- **Beds:** simple rooms or dorms with mattresses and blankets. A sleeping bag liner is a good idea.
- **Meals:** half board is standard: a home-cooked dinner (soup, bread, cheese, vegetables, often meat) and breakfast. Ask for a packed lunch for the long day to Milishevc.
- **Toilets:** shared toilets outside the huts.
- **Price:** about €25–40 per person for half board in 2026, paid in cash. See [where you sleep on the Peaks of the Balkans](/en/peaks-of-the-balkans-accommodation/) for how this compares with the villages.

There are only a few guesthouses, so book by phone or WhatsApp well ahead for July and August. Local operators warn that bookings can get mixed up in peak season; confirm a few days before you arrive. If you carry a tent, ask your guesthouse whether you can pitch it nearby; the animals and their guard dogs are a good reason not to camp just anywhere.

## Getting there: from Çerem

The usual way in is stage 3 of the loop, from Çerem. It is about 17 km with roughly 1,100 m of climbing and 500 m of descent, and takes about 6 hours. The route climbs through beech forest and pastures, passes the seasonal shepherd settlement of Balqinë and a summer coffee hut, and briefly touches Montenegrin territory near a border marker before dropping into the Dobërdol valley. See [all ten stages day by day](/en/peaks-of-the-balkans-stages/) for how this fits the full loop.

## Moving on: Dobërdol to Milishevc

The next day is one of the big ones. About an hour after leaving Dobërdol you reach a pass at roughly 2,170–2,210 m on the flank of the three-border point, where Albania, Kosovo and Montenegro meet. From there the trail follows the high ridge and descends via the shepherd settlement of Roshkodol and a dirt track to Milishevc, in Kosovo. Count on about 18 km, around 900–1,000 m of climbing and 8 hours.

There is also a branch from this stage towards Babino Polje in Montenegro, but most hikers stay on the main loop through Kosovo.

Because you cross a border away from any official checkpoint, you need the border permits for Albania, Kosovo and Montenegro in advance. See [the permits guide](/en/peaks-of-the-balkans-permits/).

## Side trip: Gjeravica

Gjeravica (2,656 m) is the highest mountain in Kosovo and stands only a few kilometres from Dobërdol. Fit hikers can climb it from Dobërdol on a long loop of about 19 km and 8–9 hours, via the Ali Peja pass (about 2,275 m), a small heart-shaped lake and the summit ridge. The return can pass Tropoja Lake and the Gacafer pastures, where Gacaferi Guest House has around 50 beds. This crosses into Kosovo too, so the same permits apply, and it is only sensible in good weather.

## Practical tips

- **Cash:** there is no ATM anywhere near Dobërdol. Bring enough euros for the guesthouse, meals and drinks.
- **Water:** fill up at springs on the way from Çerem and ask your hosts about drinking water.
- **Weather:** at this altitude evenings are cold, even in August, and storms can build up over the ridge in the afternoon. Start early on the day to Milishevc.
- **Dogs:** the sheepdogs guard their flocks. Keep your distance, walk calmly and don't cut through a herd.
- **Luggage:** horses and mules are used to carry goods to Dobërdol; ask a guesthouse in advance if you want your bag carried.
